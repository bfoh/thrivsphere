# Practice resources: supervision sheet and worksheets

The owner sent three Word documents for staff to use with clients:

- the Supervision & Next Session Planning Sheet;
- Worksheet 5: Understanding the Intensity of My Emotions;
- the Worksheet 5 Practitioner Guide.

All three are now built into the website. This page covers where to find
them, how they are kept safe, what was changed from the Word versions (for the
owner to approve), and how to add the next worksheet.

## Where to find them

| What | Where |
|---|---|
| Start a supervision sheet | **Resources** → *Start a supervision sheet*. Alternatively, open the client's record, or use *Supervision sheet* next to a past session under **Appointments**. |
| Finish a draft sheet | The client's record → *Supervision & session planning*, or **Resources** → *Recent supervision sheets*. |
| Your preparation tasks | **Resources** → *My preparation tasks*. The count also shows on the Dashboard. |
| Set a worksheet for a client | The client's record → *Worksheets* → *Set a worksheet*. |
| Worksheets clients have sent back | **Resources** → *Worksheets awaiting review*. The count also shows on the Dashboard, and the person who set the worksheet gets an email. |
| Practitioner guide | **Resources** → *Worksheet library* → *Practitioner guide*. |
| Paper copies | **Resources** → *Print a blank sheet*, or *Printable copy* in the worksheet library. |
| Clients | Their account → **Worksheets**. The overview also shows a card when a worksheet is waiting. |

## How it works

**Supervision sheet**

1. Start a sheet from the session it is about. Save a draft at any point; any
   member of staff can carry it on.
2. *Finalise* checks the sheet and then locks it. Three answers are required:
   the main issue, section 3 (safety), and the key decision. If section 3
   flags a concern, it also needs the details, who will act and by when.
3. After finalising, each row of "Who will prepare what?" becomes a task on
   that person's Resources page. Ticking a task done does not unlock the
   sheet.
4. Corrections to a finalised sheet are added as amendments with a reason, as
   with session notes. Amending needs a supervisor, admin or founder. A draft
   can be deleted by the person who started it or by a supervisor. A
   finalised sheet cannot be deleted.
5. If two people edit the same draft at once, the second save is refused with
   a message, rather than one quietly overwriting the other.

**Worksheets**

1. A practitioner sets a worksheet from the client's record, with an optional
   short note. The client is emailed that "something new" is in their account.
   The email does not say what it is, and it is not sent if the client has
   turned emails off.
2. The client fills it in, in their account. Their answers save as they go and
   stay private to them until they press *Send*. After sending, the answers
   can't be changed.
3. If the client would rather do it in a session, or on paper, the
   practitioner can choose *Enter answers with the client* instead. The record
   shows who entered them. This option is not offered once the client has
   started the worksheet themselves.
4. When a worksheet comes back, staff read it and press *Mark as reviewed*. A
   worksheet that hasn't been sent can be withdrawn.

## How the information is kept safe

- Everything is stored in the client's record in the database, in the same
  way as session notes. It is encrypted at rest and in transit, and nothing is
  kept on anyone's own device.
- Every view, save, finalise, amendment, print and refused attempt is written
  to the **Activity** log, with who did it and when. The log never contains
  the answers themselves.
- Practitioners, supervisors, admins and the founder can all see every sheet
  and worksheet. Only supervisors and above can amend a finalised sheet.
  Clients see only their own worksheets, never supervision sheets.
- Emails never contain names or answers, only a link to sign in.
- A printed supervision sheet shows the client's initials and a reference, not
  their name.
- Each record keeps the form version it was filled in against. If the wording
  changes later, old records still show the questions that were actually
  asked.

## Changes from the Word documents: for the owner to approve

The owner's wording is kept throughout unless it is listed here. Each change
follows published good practice.

### Supervision & Next Session Planning Sheet

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| The header (client, session, practitioner, supervision date) is filled in from the system. Added "Who took part in this discussion". | Fewer mistakes, and the sheet is linked to the right session and record. |
| A note at the top: write factually and briefly, because clients can ask to see their records. | NMC Code §10 and BACP guidance on records; UK GDPR subject access. |
| §1: added "Between-session practice agreed last time: Completed / Partly / Not yet / None", plus "What helped or got in the way?". | Practice is more likely to continue when it is reviewed at the next session (Kazantzis and colleagues). |
| §1: "Activity / intervention used" became "Activity / approach used". | "Intervention" sounds clinical; ThriveSphere is a non-clinical service. |
| §1: added an optional "Client's own rating of progress towards their goal (0–10)". | A simple, personal progress measure, asked of the client, not estimated. |
| §2: "Has the client's priority or goal changed?" became Yes / No, with "What is it now?" when the answer is yes. Added a reminder to keep what was said separate from interpretation. | Easier to scan, and a fairer record. |
| §3: "No new concern" can't be ticked together with a concern. Any concern needs details, who will act and by when. Crisis numbers appear when a risk is ticked, with links to raise a safeguarding concern, record a referral, or check the Scope of Practice page. | A concern written down with no owner is easy to lose. |
| §4: added "Worksheet from the library" alongside "Workbook page / visual / resource needed". Added guidance on agreeing between-session practice. | Links planning to the worksheets that can be set. |
| §5: each task has a person (picked from staff) and an optional date. After finalising it becomes a tracked task. | Replaces the paper "Done ☐" column with something that can be followed up. |
| §6: added "Is a progress review due? Not yet / At the next session / Now". | Prompts regular reviews. |
| New §7, Practitioner Reflection (optional): one learning point, and support you'd like. | Supervision traditionally has three functions: normative, formative and restorative (Proctor). The original sheet covered the first two. The section warns that colleagues can read it, so personal matters belong with the supervisor directly. |

### Worksheet 5 (client)

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| The Name and Date fields were removed. | The system records both. |
| Added a "How to use this" box: skip anything; pick a manageable situation; answers are private until sent; limits of confidentiality; the worksheet isn't monitored between sessions. | Trauma-informed practice: choice, safety and transparency (OHID, 2022). |
| §1: "What emotion did you experience?" offers emotion words to tick, plus "Or describe it in your own words". "What happened?" asks for just the facts. | Naming a feeling helps reduce its intensity (Lieberman et al., 2007). |
| §2: optional "For me, a 2 / 5 / 9 feels like…". | 0–10 ratings are personal; your own reference points make them more consistent. |
| §3: added "What did you actually do?" after "What did you feel like doing?". | Separates the urge from the action, as in the DBT model of emotions. |
| §5: the Overwhelming row lists 999 or A&E, NHS 111 option 2, Samaritans 116 123, and text SHOUT to 85258. | Current UK crisis routes, written where they are needed. |
| §6: up to three practice entries instead of one. | Practising more than once helps. |

### Worksheet 5 Practitioner Guide

The owner's text is kept word for word. Added:

- a short evidence summary;
- timing (about 20–30 minutes);
- when not to use the worksheet, or when to stop;
- a script for introducing it;
- adaptations: difficulty naming emotions, neurodivergence, literacy and language, culture;
- ThriveSphere's escalation steps;
- what to record in the session note.

All added wording is original. The DBT handouts the approach draws on are
copyrighted.

**To approve or change wording:** tell the developer which line to change. A
reworded form is added as a new version, and records already made keep the
old wording.

## Adding the next worksheet (developer notes)

1. Add `src/lib/forms/templates/worksheet-N-v1.ts`, modelled on
   `worksheet-5-v1.ts`. The template is plain data: sections; questions
   (`text`, `textarea`, `rating`, `choice`, `checkboxes`, `repeat`, …);
   guidance (`info`); and reference tables (`table`).
2. Register it in `src/lib/forms/registry.ts` and add it to `WORKSHEET_LIBRARY`
   in `src/lib/forms/library.ts`.
3. If it has a practitioner guide, add it under `src/lib/forms/guides/` and
   register it in `GUIDES`.
4. Run `npm test`. The registry tests check the following:
   - ids are unique;
   - conditions point at real questions;
   - the library and registry agree.

To reword a live template, copy it to a new version (for example `-v2.ts`)
and register both. **Never edit a version that already has answers.**
